1. 场景与算法原理说明

      
A Cornell box — the 1984 radiosity test scene — rendered by unidirectional
        path tracing on the GPU. Every pixel fires camera rays into the room, scatters
        them off diffuse, mirror and dielectric surfaces, and estimates direct light at
        each diffuse hit by sampling the ceiling emitter (next-event estimation), with
        Russian-roulette termination after the third bounce. Samples accumulate into a
        32-bit float buffer, so the image refines rather than restarts. Drag the image
        to orbit, scroll to dolly.

    

    

      
2. 性能指标计算逻辑

      
Each batch is timed on the CPU around a gl.finish() plus a one-texel
        readback — finish alone is advisory on some drivers, and a readback cannot
        complete until the pipeline has actually drained, so the figure is GPU execution
        time rather than command submission. The benchmark loop is driven by a
        message-channel yield rather than requestAnimationFrame, so it is never
        clamped to your display's refresh rate. Ray counts are not estimated: the shader
        writes per-pixel camera, shadow and bounce-ray tallies to a second render target,
        which is summed on the GPU by a log-4 reduction and read back once per second.
        Runs discard a warm-up period and auto-size the batch to about 12 ms.
        Resolution is fixed per workload and independent of window size, so runs are
        comparable across machines.

    

    

      
3. 测试结果解读指南

      
Rel. error is the estimated relative standard error of the pixel
        estimates — path tracing converges as 1/√N, so it falls slowly by design. The
        Noise and Rays views false-colour those same per-pixel buffers on a
        single-hue scale: dark is low, amber is high.
